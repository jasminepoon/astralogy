# Capability discovery prototype and copy decisions

## Second pass: hierarchy and simulation recommendation

User feedback: the first concept did not make it obvious what to click or try first. This pass supersedes the original top-stage-navigation layout and action sequencing described below.

- One primary cyan action in a recommendation panel leads each moment. The spatial result remains adjacent; current balances are quiet persistent context; detailed evidence is collapsed.
- The first action is **Try a push**, labelled **TRY FIRST**, with **Free preview · no fuel spent**. A quieter **Ask Astra instead** preserves freedom to skip the suggestion. The concept button reveals an illustrative push; the actual app still needs real drag guidance.
- After trying, the action becomes **Find a route with Astra** with its six-credit investigation price. No home answer is revealed before this step.
- Astra's located result exposes the budget obstacle. Its gravity recommendation then offers **Simulate this route**. Only after that action do the predicted route and arrival balances appear, marked as a preview with no fuel spent.
- The simulated result guides the next question: it reaches home with little fuel remaining. **Can we use less fuel?** is primary; **Run now** remains available. The revision shows the alternative and allows the faster route to be restored.
- A quiet Try → Simulate → Revise → Travel indicator explains the sequence. The old equally prominent scene-jump buttons move to collapsed concept-review controls.
- On narrow displays, the next-action panel moves above the scene so the instruction and button are encountered first.

This is a recommendation/simulation teaching concept, not an instruction to add mandatory confirmations to the delegated live flow. The concept holds states for review. Production should preserve automatic execution under existing delegation, interruption, truthful quote state, and free simulation when that is what the engine supports. No model or physics runs in this concept.

Refinement criterion: without a walkthrough, a viewer can identify the recommended first action, predict whether it spends resources, find its visual result, and identify the next useful question. These are stronger checks than whether the screen merely looks simpler. This pass remains isolated pending user review.

## The experience being tested

The person first lacks a usable home direction. Astra's investigation establishes it, but a direct push exceeds the budget. Astra reveals a feasible gravity edit. The person understands that the environment itself can be changed, then asks for a different tradeoff and sees both the route and its consequences change.

The meaningful gain is a newly understandable and usable operation. The interface must make a person able to ask and judge something more specific than “get me home.” Arrival alone does not establish this gain.

## Prototype decisions and internal refinement

1. Replaced the generic capability slogan with an explicit contrast: direct push plus braking costs about 45 fuel, available fuel is 22, and the gravity route costs 19.96. The comparison describes this preset; it must not be hardcoded into live outcomes or claim every direct maneuver is impossible.
2. Made the discovery beat visible before the field control. “Show me how” in the concept explores the reveal; it must not become an extra required permission during delegated execution. The production reveal can unfold under existing delegation.
3. Kept a short human-authored action, “Can we use less fuel?”, alongside the route. Both this action and the concept field control switch between the same two illustrated proposals. It demonstrates the intended shared representation, not the live engine's actual drag semantics.
4. Made fuel and lifetime remaining at arrival the two primary numbers. Preserve total trip cost and ETA beneath them, including braking. Revised proposal adds the delta: 5.23 fuel saved for 176.6 additional years. Both results are rounded from recorded engine checks.
5. Removed a proposed mandatory “Keep this route” confirmation during internal review. The concept's top review stages are for inspecting scenes, not controls to copy into the app. Live delegation retains its interruptible preview and autonomous execution.
6. Replaced “Change the field. Change what's possible” with “A route within reach.” Let the changed path demonstrate possibility instead of making the headline explain the thesis.
7. Changed “A push won't get us there” to “Fuel is the problem,” avoiding an unqualified physical impossibility claim where affordability is the real constraint.

The concept pauses for review; it has no live calls or timed auto-commit. Its route curves and dialogue are illustrative. Only two recorded endpoint costs are shown; there is no invented continuous cost interpolation. The final outcome is an example state, not a new run. It preserves an unbranded working title, The Way Home, pending a naming decision.

| “Gross spending cap”, recovered resources not renewing authority | Shorten primary label, retain rule in budget details | “Astra's budget” plus visible editable caps; keep used versus available distinction where consequential. Do not change authorization behavior. |

| “Use less fuel” duplicated in companion and proposal | Preserve one | One actual visible accessible control, beside the proposal while it exists. |
| Busy, revising, Interrupt/Resume, rejected request | Preserve | These communicate agency and prevent stale or unintended execution. Remove no state needed to act safely. |
| “Across the years”, “Along the computed path”, checkpoint explanation | Reduce | One travel state and progress/ETA. “Travel accelerated” when applicable. |
| “We made it”, “Home”, “The world can be quiet again” together | Consolidate | “Home.” plus arrival/braking status and actual receipt. |

Rule: preserve copy that changes what the player can understand, choose or expect from the next action. Remove text that repeats the current screen, narrates implementation, or announces the intended emotional effect.

## Review against our learnings

- One stable scene: retained across unknown, located, feasible, revised and arrived states.
- Evidence must enable action: location reveal precedes the affordability problem and field possibility.
- Human revision must change consequences: the same route control and fuel/time summary change together.
- Fewer routine approvals: review navigation is explicitly prototype-only; no new mandatory approval is proposed for the app.
- Real state beats attractive illustration: this mockup is labelled a concept, and its curves cannot prove physical fidelity. The existing engine remains authoritative for integration.
- “Achieve more” needs observable evidence: success is the viewer identifying the new action and making a more informed request, not repeating our slogan.

## What to refine with the user before integration

Review the concept without narration. At Discover, can the viewer explain the obstacle and the new possibility? At Shape, can they identify what can be manipulated? At Revise, can they explain what they gained and gave up? Then ask what they would want to try next, without suggesting the answer.

If the viewer only remembers “press a cheaper button,” the discovery beat is still insufficient. Improve the visible relation among handle, field and path before adding prose or new features. A free-form follow-up in the real app can provide stronger evidence than the shortcut, but only if it changes supported state; do not fabricate new capability to satisfy the question.

Unresolved: saved fuel has limited demonstrated value after arrival. Present the slower route as a person's preference, not an objectively superior choice. Do not add hazards to force its value during this copy pass.
